# Player critique — Resonant

This is a reading of the game *from inside it*: what a person with four dials
and no design document actually experiences. It is not a review of the
architecture. The architecture is unusually coherent. The play is not yet as
coherent as the architecture.

Read `IDEAL_PLAY.md` next for what a session should feel like and which
changes would buy that without breaking the premise.

---

## 1. What the player thinks this is

The boot screen says: you cannot move; you can only change what is rendered to
you. Then it names four Greek letters. Then it recommends headphones.

That is an honest pitch, and it is also a wall. A player who has never opened
the README does not know that φ is a spectrum, that a “smudge” is a node, that
holding four needles on a drifting signature *is* the game, or that the
beautiful solar layer is gated behind buying Locomotion with insight they do
not yet have.

What they *do* see, after BEGIN:

- A dark field with drifting lights.
- Four circular gauges along the bottom.
- A spectrum strip they will tap before they understand it.
- One line of objective: “Sweep φ until a smudge resolves, then hold the lock.”
- A hamburger of panels they do not need yet.

The first ten minutes are a test of whether they will treat the dials as an
instrument or as unexplained HUD. The game is excellent for the first group
and nearly silent for the second.

The live guide (`?`) is the right idea — it describes the situation in front
of you, not the game in the abstract — but it lives behind a menu, and the
skills that matter (drag a knob in a circle; swing wide for fine control; two
thumbs on two dials; listen to the beat) are the ones a confused player will
not think to look up.

**Player verdict on the opening:** the fantasy is clear; the first verb is
not. “Sweep φ” is not an action a thumb already knows.

---

## 2. The hold is the real game, and it is good

Once a lock lands, Resonant does something most idle/exploration games never
do: it asks you to *stay*. Coherence fills. The tone climbs. The ring
tightens. Strike is a push on a hold you are already winning, timed to a
window the essence already taught you, not a second game bolted on.

That loop — sweep, resolve, hold, strike-in-the-window, crystallise — is the
one thing a player will remember. It has mass. The needles overshoot. Detents
tick. Fine control by distance from the hub is the best control in the file,
and almost nobody is told it exists.

What goes wrong around it:

- **The wait is still a wait.** Strike exists because two to six seconds of
  perfect alignment is dead air. It helps. It does not make the hold *legible*
  to someone who has not yet felt a crystallise. The first lock should be
  louder than every later one — visually, sonically, and in the objective —
  because it is the moment the game teaches its own verb.
- **Layers look like wallpaper.** The code’s claim is that primitives change
  the *rules* of the field. The player’s claim, early on, is that they changed
  the *colour*. Gate windows, twins, and order-chains are the actual
  differences, and they only become visible after the player already knows to
  look for them. A ghost layer (in reach, will not cohere) is the one
  difference that currently reads at a glance. More of the game should read
  like that.
- **The upgrade shop arrives too fast.** After the first crystal the
  objective becomes “buy FOCUS” or “buy RANGE”. That is true and it is also
  the moment the game starts feeling like a number. Touching a new layer is
  supposed to be a campaign. The first two purchases should feel like opening
  an instrument, not topping up a skill tree.

Protect the hold. Do not add a third way to skip it. Strike already exists.
World pulse already exists. A third clicker would teach the player that
waiting is a problem to be engineered around, which is the opposite of this
game.

---

## 3. Six pathways, one obvious road

On paper there are six routes: TUNE, REACH, CONTACT, INWARD, BEYOND,
RECOGNITION. In the first hour there is one: hold nodes, buy φ range, hold
more nodes. The others are real, but they are not *visible as choices*.

| Pathway | What the player actually meets | Why it fails to feel like a route |
|---|---|---|
| **TUNE** | The only loop that works with zero extra knowledge | Fine. This should stay the spine. |
| **REACH** | A research list in a drawer, starting at Locomotion (120 Ψ) | “Build a body” is a sentence, not a desire, until they have seen a world they cannot touch. |
| **CONTACT** | Roughly one civilisation per 190 systems, then a carrier lock, then awareness | Rarity is the point. Unfindability is not. Rumours exist; they do not yet *lead*. |
| **INWARD** | Buy Σ range down, then four small scopes that still play as holds | The “only here” of each scope is a multiplier and a paragraph, not a moment. |
| **BEYOND** | Buy Σ range up, scrub cosmic time, swap laws | The ensemble is the best idea in the game and it is behind a shop. |
| **RECOGNITION** | The codex spreadsheet | The foresight ghosts are the reward. The sheet is the map. Most players will never open it on purpose. |

A pathway the player cannot name from inside the game is not a pathway. It is
content they will stumble into or miss. The Pathways panel tells the truth;
the world does not yet *perform* it.

## 4. The solar layer: a second game that starts in the ocean

Descending is the one navigation in the game, and it is the right one. Map →
system → globe is readable. The astrophysics chain is the rare case of flavour
text that is also the mechanic: a world is airless because it is small and
hot, and you can see that.

Then you take a body, and the game changes character.

**What works**

- The modal split (observing scrubs time; inhabiting is stuck in the present)
  is the best thematic rule in the project. Players who get it will quote it.
- Wrong body, named reason (“needs a surface”, “cannot operate in liquid”) is
  honest. Most games lie here.
- Pulse-as-survey is the right cousin of strike: attention on a patch, not a
  farm. Diminishing per world is correct.
- Extractors that actually pay idle Ψ finally match the blurb that they are
  the structure that pays for itself.

**What it feels like**

- The first landable world is often *ocean*. Walkers refuse. The mote works
  everywhere and looks like nothing. The player is told to take a body, takes
  the wrong one, and the surface is a green sky over a flat teal slab. The
  README’s vegetation, tides, and fall-line slides are real — on land, in the
  right camera, after the right embark. The first screenshot is water.
- Side-on on water is a horizon line. Freeroam is a 48×32 grid of rectangles.
  The globe is the most “planet-like” view, and inhabiting *leaves* it. So the
  fantasy of standing on a world and the picture of standing on a world
  disagree for a long time.
- Tap now means three different things: strike a node in the field, survey a
  seam on a planet, pick a star on the map. The objective line usually names
  the right one. The cursor does not.
- `C` / scene tag for camera is correct (walking is not a mode switch). Almost
  nobody will press C unless told. Altitude auto-switching to the map at 0.22
  will feel like a bug the first time it happens.

The embodied half is a second game sharing four dials. That is the design.
The player needs one *successful* walk — land underfoot, a hill, a creature,
a named biome — before they will believe it.

## 5. Contact is the best content and the worst hunt

Talking to a civilisation by tuning onto its carrier is the game’s thesis
made playable: everything you learned holding layers is the apparatus for
meeting someone. Awareness running the other way — they have to notice you —
is the only time the universe acts first. Listen / charts / trade / gift /
teaching / uplift are real consequences, not flavour.

The hunt is the problem. Civilisations are rare by construction. The
consciousness field starts tiny. Dim stars are unnamed lights. An amber ring
is the rarest marker, and you will not see one unless you already know to
look for it and have expanded reach.

So the best writing in the game is behind a search that most playthroughs
will not complete without treating the star map as a grind. Rumours between
cultures already exist. They do not yet pull a player across the map the way
a named neighbour on a chart should.

Help is not neutral (uplift welcomed or resented by disposition). That is a
design sentence players will feel *if they get that far*. Most will not.

## 6. Insight, idle, and the risk of becoming a clicker

Two currencies is the right number. Insight is spent activity. Gnosis is
unspent understanding and pays everywhere. That split is the fractal premise
as an economy, and it should stay.

What has accumulated around it:

- Passive Ψ from bands and tiers, so returning is always worth something.
- Strike combo, logarithmic, capped — a push, not a skip.
- World pulse, diminished per planet, two numbers, no cell map.
- Extractors, matured over ~20 minutes of play, now actually on the idle
  floor.

Each of those is justified. Together they teach a dangerous lesson: Ψ arrives
while you are not holding. The hold is still the best Ψ/minute if you are
good. Idle is the most *visible* Ψ/minute if you are not. A player who opens
the game, taps a planet, and watches +6.6/s will not go back to four-dimensional
locks unless the lock feels better than the number.

Extractor maturity is invisible. You site a seam, the world looks the same,
and twenty minutes later a rate ticks. Structures that “bias a rate” are
philosophically correct and dramatically empty. The player wants to see the
pit, the glow, the tanker — or at least the HUD naming *this world* as the
source.

Pulse on an observing tap (aim latitude *and* pay) is easy to miss as a
survey and easy to spam as a farm until diminishing returns kick in. The
first pulse of a world already toasts. Later pulses are a click. That is
fine if the click is rare. It is not fine if it is the loop.

## 7. Presentation: too much science, not enough situation

The HUD is an instrument panel. That is a compliment and a warning. Four
dials, a warped spectrum strip, a progress percentage that mixes bands,
tiers, gnosis, research and worlds into one bar nobody can parse, Greek
letters, kelvin, bars, g, AU, Gyr, φ centres, essence names, primitive
glyphs, vessel archetypes.

`showSci` defaults on. The live readout is often the most interesting
sentence on screen (“Take a body to touch this world”) sitting under a stack
of numbers that do not change the next action.

Scopes that are “places, not minigames” still *look* like the field with a
different shader. The player who learned Gate in Electromagnetic will, in
theory, read a rhythm in cytoplasm. In practice they will see a new
background and hunt the same smudge. The only-here of cytoplasm (expression
changing a biosphere from inside) is a number on a later orbit. The only-here
of the foam (nothing persists, including your body) is a refusal message.

Bloom, arrival zoom, silence in vacuum, pressure-scaled wind — these are the
presentation choices that already feel like the game. They should be the
standard: one sensory fact per scope that you cannot get anywhere else.

---

## 8. What is already excellent (do not sand off)

Keep these even when they inconvenience onboarding:

- Four dials as the whole interface; bodies reinterpret them; no second
  scheme.
- Alignment as a product of Gaussians; demand ramps with band. That *is* the
  tutorial.
- Ghost layers: visible, not holdable.
- Strike cannot finish a node; fatigue exists so clicking is not a skip.
- Observing vs inhabiting as a modal split on time vs space.
- Contact by carrier, not a Hail button.
- `effective = derived ⊕ deltas`. Tiny saves, exact time, no heightmaps.
- Six primitives rather than twelve minigames. Knowledge transfer is the
  long game.
- The test suite as specification. A red suite means the game is wrong.

The critique is not “make it more like a normal game.” It is: the player
cannot yet *perform* the game that is already there.

---

## 9. Short verdict

Resonant is a finished simulation of a design, and an unfinished game of
that design.

The hold, the dials, the ladder, and contact-by-tuning are a complete thesis.
The first hour does not teach the thesis. The solar layer does not show the
thesis until you are already committed. The six pathways are a map in a
drawer. Idle and pulse are beginning to compete with the thing the audio and
the needles were built for.

The work that remains is not more systems. It is making the systems the
player already has *happen to them* in an order they can feel.
